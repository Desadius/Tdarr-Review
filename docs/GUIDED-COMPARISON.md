# Guided comparison

The in-app guided comparison is the default media-first review workflow: a single pass over three excerpts at 2, 50 and 95 % of the film. From 1200 px wide it opens full screen; the picture fills the review, and the heading, tabs and film strip slide in at the window edges or with Escape. One shared context keeps the selected pair, times, listening mode and current excerpt together. The excerpt times are the whole seconds the server already prepares in the background (Detail video profile), so a prepared film starts without waiting; while a film is open, background preparation continues with the films after it in the strip.

## Playback modes

Each excerpt is bounded to 12 seconds and plays **once**, then the player stops and waits. The default **Switch** mode (`guided-switch`) starts both copies at the same time on the `AudioContext` clock; only one is heard, and A changes which one with a short gain ramp, without restarting or moving the playhead. Guided ears (original left, together, result right: three phases per excerpt) and Sequential (original, then result) remain as options under **Tracks and times**. Changing the mode is a changed comparison context and clears the guided picture, sound and synchronisation checks, as before.

The player can pause visibly while video is waiting, after an error, or when the browser suspends its media context. Resume, a repeat (R) or another excerpt is always an explicit action. The workflow does not silently normalize audio or align offsets.

## Confirmations and saving

Preparation is confirmed once with Enter on the start prompt. After each excerpt a prompt over the dimmed picture asks for the picture (1), the sound (2) and that excerpt's synchronisation (3); Enter confirms whatever is still open in that excerpt and plays the next one. Picture and sound need one confirmation in any excerpt, synchronisation one per excerpt (`syncStart`, `syncMiddle`, `syncEnd`). Playback, clip completion and technical readiness never set a check, and nothing is set before a key or click.

Each confirmation is saved immediately: one review-session write per confirmation, in the order preparation → picture → HDR → audio → synchronisation, each with the expected revision (compare-and-swap). The server keeps a check only once the checks of the earlier steps are saved, so a confirmation made out of order waits in the browser (chip state *confirmed*) and is written as soon as it can be. Each synchronisation observation is bound to its excerpt time; picture and sound to the excerpt in which they were confirmed. A conflict stops the queue, keeps the remaining confirmations as a draft and offers **Load saved state**. Reopening a film continues with the first excerpt whose synchronisation is not confirmed. Saving a review never approves or replaces a film.

After the last excerpt the decision prompt shows the potential savings and the track pair: S **Approve**, Shift+S **Approve and replace** (the existing confirmation dialog), Z **Reject** (the existing dialog with a reason), N **Next movie**. X opens a note with **Defer review**. An HDR/Dolby Vision film names its device on the start prompt and asks for the native-device HDR confirmation (D) before the decision; the named-device rule of at least two characters still applies. The external-player/TV route keeps its separate-step flow. A review saved before guided metadata existed keeps its evidence when it is opened again.

## Controls and display limits

A small key legend stays in a corner of the picture; ? opens the full help, which also holds the explanations and preview limits. Space/K play or pause, A original ↔ transcode, W divider 50 % / automatic sweep / off (off shows the copy being heard), ←/→ seek, [ ] previous/next excerpt, 1 2 3, Enter, R, X, S, Shift+S, Z, N as above, T tracks and times, F fullscreen, M mute, H hides the overlay and Esc closes help, the note or the options and then shows the movie details. Shortcuts are scoped to the focused review and never act in editable fields. Both waveforms run mirrored as a translucent 60 px strip over the top of the picture (original up, transcode down) with one cursor; clicking it seeks. While an excerpt is not ready, only a thin progress line shows in the picture.

Fit, Stretch and Fill are local browser preferences applied equally to both images. 1:1 frames display native source pixels; stepping is approximate for variable-frame-rate material. Video samples are bounded SDR previews and audio is mono at 48 kHz. They do not establish native HDR fidelity, surround sound, original-resolution playback quality or codec transparency. HDR/Dolby Vision therefore still requires a separate native-device confirmation.

## Design basis

The [Web Audio specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.0/#dom-audiobuffersourcenode-start) defines start scheduling against `AudioContext.currentTime` with explicit buffer offsets. The implementation schedules both copies on that clock: in Switch mode once per excerpt with separate selection gains, in the phase modes restarting the same interval for every phase; browser video remains subject to buffering and the visible drift guard. The ear-separated sequence is a practical comparison requested by this project, not a validated listening-test standard or a whole-film quality certificate.

[W3C character-key guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/character-key-shortcuts.html) permits character shortcuts scoped to component focus. The single pass uses that approach, with native form controls retaining their usual keys. [W3C focus visibility guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html) identifies overlapping fixed controls as a focus hazard; the overlay keeps prompts centred, hides with H, and every action also has a visible button. These choices are not a general accessibility-conformance claim.
